# Voice Guide

The voice system controls language only. Candidate facts come from the canonical evidence system.

## Contexts

- `referral`: clear, broad, practical
- `startup`: direct, builder-oriented, ownership-heavy
- `mnc`: conventional, technical, ATS-readable

## Core rule

Write like a technically capable person describing work they actually did.

Prefer:
- specific nouns
- concrete verbs
- short sentences
- real technical details
- real constraints
- real decisions
- verified outcomes
- natural rhythm

## Anti-slop

Do not pad sentences with:

passionate
innovative
cutting-edge
revolutionary
visionary
world-class
transformative
next-generation
results-driven
highly motivated
proven track record
leveraged
utilized
spearheaded
orchestrated
seamless
robust
scalable

The problem is not the individual word. The problem is using it instead of evidence.

## Humanization rules

Do not:

- force every bullet into the same formula
- mechanically rotate action verbs
- make every sentence the same length
- add adjectives before technical nouns
- manufacture impact
- mirror JD sentences
- over-explain ordinary implementation
- hide uncertainty behind polished language
- write artificial "founder voice"

Natural variation is preferred.

## Bullet test

For every bullet ask:

**What happened?**

Then, where useful:

**How was it done?**

Then:

**What changed, or what constraint mattered?**

Do not force a result when none is verified.

## Ownership

Use the weakest verb that remains accurate.

used ≠ integrated
integrated ≠ implemented
implemented ≠ designed
designed ≠ owned
owned ≠ architected

For open source:

issue ≠ implementation
proposal ≠ merge
approval ≠ merge
downstream adoption ≠ authorship
draft ≠ shipped

## Zinsser test

Before final output:

- remove words that add no meaning
- replace abstract language with the concrete thing
- cut redundant phrases
- prefer short words where they stay precise
- preserve unusual technical details when they are true
- stop editing when the sentence becomes less natural

## Track-specific writing

### Referral

Make the candidate understandable to a general recruiter or contact who may forward the document without technical context.

### Startup

Show what was built, what was owned, and what technical decisions were made.

Do not turn the candidate into a founder biography.

### MNC

Use standard terminology, precise technical nouns, and concise evidence.

Do not write for an imaginary ATS score.

## Final human test

Read the resume once without knowing who wrote it.

Ask:

Would a real engineer write this sentence this way?

If not, simplify it.

**Evidence over adjectives. Specificity over polish. Clarity over performance.**
